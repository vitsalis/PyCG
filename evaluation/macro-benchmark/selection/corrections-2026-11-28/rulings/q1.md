# Q1. einops: generated open path families and their weight

Adopted by the lead in place of a referee, maintainer's instruction of
2026-11-28. Read at `c7fc8fb3`. Paths are relative to
`evaluation/macro-benchmark/`; a package's files are cited from its root
under `projects/<name>/`, its ground truth is under `ground-truth/`, and
`benchmark-rules.md:<n>` is a line of `evaluation/benchmark-rules.md` at
that commit.

**1. The question.** A study of a tool's errors counts 1,479 outside false
negatives in einops, 37% of the 3,994 summed outside false negatives. It
asks (a) whether a generated lower-bound family is a valid ground truth,
and (b) whether one project should weigh that much in the summed outside
score.

What the ground truth holds (einops v0.8.2, `8e911db`), counted from
`einops.ledger` and `einops.json`:

| Measure | Count |
|---|---:|
| Outside graph pairs of einops | 1,833 (of 22,494 outside pairs in the 60 graphs, 8.1%) |
| ... whose callee is a missing library (torch, jax, cupy, tensorflow, paddle, oneflow, tinygrad, pytensor, mlx, keras, flax) | 1,516 |
| ... at the 12 sites declared under "Open path sets" (`einops.md:179-202`) | 1,001 |
| ... of those, members with a repeated segment (n = 2, the lower-bound witnesses) | 284 |
| ... numpy scalar class names (`numpy.<scalar>.reshape`, all at `_backends.py:96:25`) | 22 |
| ... confirmed by the trace (numpy backend only) | 167 |

The study's false negatives sit mostly at `_backends.py:96:25`
`x.reshape(shape)` in `AbstractBackend.reshape` (the site has 469
callees), `102:37` `getattr(x, operation)(axis=axes)` (a finite family
`D`, not an open site), `641:25`, `648:38`, `509:42`, `591:25`, `255:42`
and `_torch_specific.py:74:25`; about 1,000 of the 1,479 are at the 12
open sites.

So the weight does not come mainly from the open (unbounded) part: only
284 pairs are n = 2 witnesses. It comes from the stub-path rule (every
composition of backend operations on a missing-library tensor is its own
name: `jax.numpy.stack.reshape.transpose.min.reshape`) times eleven
backends, and those members are finite and each one is a possible callee.

**2. The ruling.** **Apply the open-path rule as written; the report must
carry it.** A generated family is a valid ground truth when each member
is a callee on an admitted configuration and the open sites are declared;
generation by script is how the members are listed, not evidence for
them. The rule's "read with and without each such package" is a
requirement of the published results: the outside row is given for the
60 packages and for the packages without open sets (without einops,
jsonpickle and fabric). Inside results do not change (no inside edge of
einops depends on an open set).

Scope: reporting only. It does not cap a package's weight, change the
summed definition, or remove members. It does not cover the finite stub
compositions (1,516 missing-library names): their weight follows from the
stub-path rulings, and a change there would be a change of the
missing-module convention for every package (see Q3), not an einops
question.

Effect on ground truths: none (0 edges added, removed or renamed in any
package). Effect on the reporting, applied in its own change: the results
of `evaluation/README.md` gain the outside row without the three
packages, and "Scoring" of `benchmark-rules.md` names that second outside
reading. Their outside pairs: einops 1,833, jsonpickle 928, fabric 270;
3,031 of 22,494.

**3. What the rules say.**

- "Open path sets (the lead's decision; einops)", Rulings for batch 7
  (`benchmark-rules.md:2867`): "the ground truth records the members with
  one and two repetitions, declares each site in its `<name>.md` under
  'Open path sets' ..., and states the graph at those sites as a lower
  bound ... Findings at those sites are applied when verified and are not
  counted in the audit's stop rule, and the outside edges of its batch
  are read with and without each such package."
- "A stub result keeps its path through assignment (einops)"
  (`benchmark-rules.md:2859`): "A missing-library result retains its
  access path through assignment, storage in package-created containers,
  and forwarding through package functions".
- "Forwarded stub paths ... (fabric Q-F1, Q-F2)", corrections of
  2026-11-25 (`benchmark-rules.md:3155`): the same open-set treatment for
  recursion, "longer verified members stay valid additions".
- "One name per admitted concrete receiver class (jsonpickle)"
  (`benchmark-rules.md:2559`) and "Architectures of an admitted operating
  system (einops)" (`benchmark-rules.md:2902`) give the 22 numpy scalar
  names.
- Scoring (`benchmark-rules.md:24`): "precision, recall and F1 add the
  counts of every project first".

The rules decide both parts: (a) yes; (b) the rule already answers it,
and the published report does not apply it. Every recorded member is a
callee that Python can reach on an admitted configuration; the n = 2
members are concrete edges, not placeholders. The graph is incomplete at
12 sites (no n >= 3), which the rule declares. For (b), the rule asks for
the outside edges to be read with and without each package with open
sets. The results table of `evaluation/README.md` (`:56-62`) and the
scoring text (`benchmark-rules.md:24-37`) report only the summed outside
row; no file of the repository splits it (searched for "open path",
"open receiver", "lower bound" outside the ground truths). The packages
with open sets are einops, jsonpickle and fabric (the other `## Open path
sets` sections, in batch 8, say "None.").

**4. The evidence.**

- `_backends.py:96` `AbstractBackend.reshape` returns `x.reshape(shape)`
  for every backend that inherits it (JAX, CuPy, PyTensor, MLX, Torch,
  OneFlow; `einops.md` "Open path sets" table), and its input is the
  reduction result `D`, which for Torch and OneFlow is a loop reassignment
  (`_backends.py:254-255`, `:503-509`): `x = getattr(x, operation)(dim=i)`
  over the client's reduced axes, so the number of repetitions has no
  source bound.
- `_backends.py:102` `getattr(x, operation)(axis=axes)` is one call with
  `operation` one of seven reductions (`einops.py:328-329`): finite.
- numpy's scalars reach `96:25` after a full reduction (`einops.md:425`).
- The families were generated by the writer's `families.py` from the
  ruling's definitions and every entry was read against the source branch
  (`einops.md:160-177`); the blind audit and Codex round 1 audited them.

**5. Against.** A summed score in which one package supplies 37% of the
false negatives of a row measures one modelling feature (stub-path chains
over many missing frameworks) more than the population of packages; the
split removes only 3,031 pairs and leaves the finite stub chains in the
first row. A reading robust to that is a per-package (macro-averaged)
score, which is a scoring decision outside the ground-truth rules, not an
audit correction.
